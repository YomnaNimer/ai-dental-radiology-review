# Draft Mini-Review

## Artificial Intelligence in Dental Radiology

Artificial intelligence is increasingly being studied in dental radiology as a tool to assist image interpretation, detection of pathology, segmentation of anatomical structures, and measurement of disease-related changes. Current research is concentrated in caries detection, periodontal bone loss assessment, periapical lesion detection, and broader oral-maxillofacial imaging applications such as CBCT interpretation.

## Caries Detection

AI models for caries detection are commonly studied on bitewing, periapical, panoramic, and other dental imaging modalities. Deep learning models are often designed to identify carious lesions, support early detection, or reduce missed findings. Recent systematic reviews suggest promising diagnostic performance, but clinical implementation still depends on external validation, representative datasets, careful integration into dental workflows, and dentist oversight.

## Periodontal Bone Loss

Periodontal applications often focus on detecting alveolar bone loss or estimating periodontal status from two-dimensional radiographs. AI may help standardize radiographic measurement and reduce variability, but studies differ in imaging type, annotation standards, disease definitions, and performance metrics. This makes direct comparison difficult and highlights the need for standardized datasets and clinical validation.

## Periapical Lesions

Periapical lesion research includes detection, segmentation, and classification on panoramic, periapical, and CBCT images. AI may support earlier recognition of periapical pathology and improve consistency in lesion assessment. However, lesion appearance varies across imaging modalities, and model performance may be affected by image quality, anatomical overlap, and differences in labeling by clinicians.

## CBCT and Oral-Maxillofacial Imaging

CBCT and oral-maxillofacial imaging provide three-dimensional information that can support diagnosis of complex anatomical and pathological findings. AI research in this area includes lesion detection, segmentation, anatomical landmark identification, and broader maxillofacial pathology assessment. These applications are clinically promising, but they also require careful validation because CBCT datasets can be smaller, more heterogeneous, and more computationally complex than two-dimensional radiographic datasets.

## Clinical Opportunities

AI may be most useful as a second-reader or decision-support tool in dental radiology. Potential benefits include:

- improved consistency in radiographic screening
- assistance with early lesion detection
- support for measurement-heavy tasks
- faster image review in busy clinical settings
- improved documentation and patient communication

## Limitations and Research Gaps

Common limitations across the literature include:

- small or single-center datasets
- inconsistent annotation methods
- limited external validation
- retrospective study designs
- unclear generalizability to everyday clinical practice
- limited reporting on bias, equity, and real-world workflow integration
- need for prospective trials and clinician-AI performance studies

## Working Conclusion

AI in dental radiology shows strong potential, especially for caries detection, periodontal bone loss assessment, periapical lesion detection, and CBCT/oral-maxillofacial imaging support. The strongest future research will likely focus on validated, explainable, clinician-supervised AI systems that improve diagnostic consistency while preserving dentist responsibility for final interpretation.
